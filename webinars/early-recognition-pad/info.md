Webinar title: Early Recognition and Treatment of Peripheral Artery Disease
registration link: https://us02web.zoom.us/webinar/register/5117822176700/WN__LQEFtd4SnCTSHvwGYvQQw#/registration
description:
Join us for this free webinar with an informative panel discussion designed for any clinician or patient featuring insights from a vascular surgeon, a primary care physician, and an interventionist on peripheral artery disease. Gain practical knowledge about early PAD detection, the relationship between PAD and cardiovascular medications, and the available revascularization treatments to guide patient referrals and enhance care delivery.

Session Objectives:
1. Describe early warning signs, risk factors, and primary care assessment methods for peripheral artery disease.
2. Emphasize the importance of PAD screening and early education when managing co-morbidities.
3. Discuss revascularization options and effective referral processes for specialist care.

Bio:
Dr. Olamide Alabi

Dr. Olamide Alabi is an Associate Professor of Surgery at Emory University School of Medicine and a board-certified vascular and endovascular surgeon at Emory University Hospital. She provides comprehensive, evidence-based care across the full spectrum of vascular disease. She serves as the Chief Quality Officer of the Division of Vascular Surgery and Endovascular Therapy for Emory Healthcare, President of the Society of Black Vascular Surgeons, Chair of the Research Committee for the American Heart Association's PAD Collaborative National Action Plan, and past Chair of the American Heart Association's Peripheral Vascular Disease Council Early Career and Fellow-in-Training Committee. As a vascular surgeon and clinical researcher/innovator, Dr. Alabi’s academic portfolio focuses on improving quality, equity, and outcomes of care for patients with peripheral artery disease. Her work integrates prospective clinical research, implementation science, and systems-level quality improvement to optimize diagnostic pathways, improve procedural decision-making, and enhance limb-preservation strategies.
